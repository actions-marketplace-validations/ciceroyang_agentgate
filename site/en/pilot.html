The mutual action plan

Every week produces evidence or a decision.

The pilot is not an open-ended product tour. The customer and AgentGate each have a defined action, deliverable, and exit point.

Week 1

Inventory and first report

You review scope and obvious omissions. We match the approved list against available public evidence and expose every failed or unmeasured check.

OutputInitial HTML report and a continue, adjust, or stop decision.

Week 2

Correct the evidence

Your reviewer identifies false positives, missed context, and the decision the evidence needs to support. We correct interpretation errors and separate customer-owned questions.

OutputReviewed findings, a gap register, and an optional policy file.

Week 3

Observe change

You confirm relevant inventory or version changes. We compare retained public-evidence snapshots without inventing history that was never captured.

OutputChange note and updated evidence record.

Week 4

Make the decision

Your team chooses what to approve, reject, remediate, monitor, or investigate. We review usefulness, accuracy, operating burden, and purchasing reality.

OutputFinal report and an explicit next-step decision.

Week 1 continuation gate

Trust boundary

Start with less access.

The default input is a shareable list of MCP servers or agent tools. Deeper evidence is optional and requires explicit approval.

What you can provide

  • Default: approved tool names.
  • Optional: redacted configuration with secrets and internal addresses removed.
  • Optional: reviewed, redacted scanner output produced inside your environment.
  • Optional: a specifically authorized read-only source subset.

What not to send

  • Passwords, tokens, API keys, or connection strings.
  • Internal addresses or material you are not authorized to share.
  • Production access or permission to run the tools under review.
  • Source code unless a specific read-only scope is approved.

What success records

Feedback is not the finish line.

A positive comment is useful, but the pilot is designed to capture operational and purchasing evidence.

Scope

The actual tools reviewed and the material boundary used.

Unknowns

Unclear identity, source, version, coverage, or ownership.

Accuracy

Confirmed false positives and important missed context.

Decision

A real approval, rejection, remediation, monitoring, or investigation action.

Reuse

Whether the evidence is used again after a relevant change.

Purchase path

Willingness to pay, the internal owner, and required prerequisites.

What the pilot can establish

  • What public evidence exists for the agreed inventory.
  • What was checked, failed, corrected, or left unmeasured.
  • Whether the record supports a real governance decision.
  • Whether the outcome is worth repeating, self-hosting, or buying.

What it does not prove

  • That a tool is secure, compliant, or approved.
  • How the tool behaves in your production environment.
  • Complete vulnerability or policy coverage.
  • Availability of SSO, RBAC, multi-tenancy, signed audit export, or SIEM integration today.
