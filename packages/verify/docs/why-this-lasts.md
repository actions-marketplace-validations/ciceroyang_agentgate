# The three conditions for a problem worth cultivating

*A filter, so that the next idea is judged before it is built.*

An engineer is drawn to the problem they can solve. That instinct is exactly wrong
here: anything a better engineer can solve, a better-funded team will solve and ship
as a feature. The problem worth a decade is the one that never gets solved, only
reduced.

Three conditions, all required:

1. **Adversarial.** Someone is actively and continuously working against you. A
   non-adversarial problem yields to better engineering. An adversarial one does not:
   the defence must be right every time, the attack only once. This is why security is
   a permanent industry and not a solved one.
2. **Cross-boundary.** No single party sees or controls the whole surface. A vendor can
   only fix what happens inside their product; the attack happens between products.
3. **Incentive-misaligned.** The party best placed to solve it does not want to, or
   cannot credibly attest to its own answer.

## What this rules out

- **"Chat with your documents."** None of the three. Absorbed, free, better by default.
- **"Add a verify button to the answer."** Touches one and three, but it is a feature
  inside somebody else's product, and it will be shipped as one. This is the current
  version of crosscheck, and it is not the thing.

## What it points at

**The integrity of what arrives at a person, or at the agent acting for them.**

- Adversarial: the content is written by someone trying to make the reader do
  something against their interest, and that someone adapts as defences improve.
- Cross-boundary: the message arrives over SMS, WeChat, a screenshot, a phone call, a
  web page, a file. No single platform sees all of it.
- Misaligned: platforms are rewarded for engagement, and a model vendor is not
  motivated to tell you that content like the content it generates cannot be trusted.

This is a position for a neutral third party, because the two parties who could hold
it cannot: one will not, and the other cannot attest to itself.

## The uncomfortable part

A permanent problem is not automatically a business. The people who suffer it have
historically not paid for its mitigation: consumer antivirus exists but is largely
free, and the money sits upstream with whoever absorbs the loss - banks, insurers,
platforms, regulators. A direction like this ends either in someone upstream paying,
or in reach without revenue.

That is a real constraint and it is written down here so it is not discovered late.
